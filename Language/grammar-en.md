## ✅ Lesson 1: Basics of Sentence Structure
Sentence = Subject + Verb (+ Object/Complement)

A sentence must have:

1. A **subject** (who or what the sentence is about)

2. A **predicate** (what the subject does or is)

Example 👉
- The dog (subject) barked loudly (predicate).


### ➡️ Types: simple, compound, complex, compound-complex.


### 1️⃣**Simple Sentence:** 

A simple sentence has **One independent clause**

Example 👉
- *I read books.*
- *The sun rises.*
- *I enjoy reading books.*
- *She studies hard for exams.*

### 2️⃣**Compound Sentence:** 

A compound sentence has **two independent clauses** joined by a coordinating conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so = **FANBOYS**) or by **a semicolon**.

Example 👉
- *I read books, and I write essays.*
- *The sun sets, and the stars appear.*
- *I wanted to go for a walk, but it started to rain.*
- *She studies hard, and she always gets good marks.*
- *He loves pizza; he eats it every weekend.*

### 3️⃣**Complex Sentence:** 

A complex sentence has **one independent clause** and **one or more dependent clauses** (cannot stand alone). 

Example 👉
- *Although I was tired, I finished my homework.*
- *She smiled because she passed the test.*
- *When the sun sets, the sky turns orange.*
- *Because I was tired, I went to sleep.*
- *Although the sun sets, the sky remains bright.*

### 4️⃣**Compound-Complex Sentence:** 

A compound-complex sentence has at least **two independent clauses** and at least **one dependent clause**.

Example 👉
- *Although I was tired, I finished my homework, and I submitted it.*
- *Although the sun sets, the sky remains bright, and the stars appear.*


### 💡Note: How to Identify Clauses
- **Independent clause:** A complete sentence → "I read books."
- **Dependent clause:** Needs more information → "Because I read books."

## ✅ Lesson 2: Parts of Speech

Every sentence in English is built from **eight essential parts of speech**. 

| Part of Speech | Example |
|----------------|---------|
| Noun           | person, place thing, idea |
| Pronoun        | he, she, it, they |
| Verb           | run, think, is |
| Adjective      | red, beautiful, large |
| Adverb         | quickly, very |
| Preposition    | in, on, under |
| Conjunction    | and, but, although |
| Interjection   | Wow! Oh no! |

Example 👉

- *The small (adj.) boy (noun) ran (verb) quickly (adv.) across the street to (prep.) his friend.*

### 1️⃣ Noun

A person, place, thing, or idea.

👉 Examples:

- Person: teacher, doctor
- Place: Australia, school
- Thing: book, phone
- Idea: happiness, freedom

👉 Sentence:

- **The student** carried a **book** to the **library.**

### 2️⃣ Pronoun

Replaces a noun to avoid repetition.

👉 Examples:

- I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them.

👉 Sentence:

- **She** loves reading because **it** relaxes **her**.

### 3️⃣ Verb

Shows action or a state of being.

👉 Examples:

- Action: run, write, jump, read

- State of being: is, are, was, were

👉 Sentence:

- The dog **barks** loudly.

- They **are** happy.

### 4️⃣ Adjective

Describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

👉 Examples:

- big, small, beautiful, blue, tall

👉 Sentence:

- The **blue** car is very fast.

### 5️⃣ Adverb

Describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb. Often ends in **-ly**.

👉 Examples:

- quickly, slowly, very, well, always

👉 Sentence:

- She runs **quickly**.

- It is **very** hot today.

### 6️⃣ Preposition

Shows relationship of a noun/pronoun to another word.

👉 Examples:

- in, on, at, over, under, beside, between, by, with

👉 Sentence:

The keys are on the table.

- I walked **through** the park.

### 7️⃣ Conjunction

Connects words, phrases, or clauses.

👉 Examples:

- Coordinating: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (FANBOYS)

- Subordinating: although, because, since, unless, while

👉 Sentence:

- I wanted coffee, **but** I drank tea.

- **Although** it was raining, we went outside.

### 8️⃣ Interjection

Expresses strong feeling or emotion.

👉 Examples:

- Wow! Oh no! Hey! Ouch! Hooray!

👉 Sentence:

- **Wow!** That was an amazing movie.

### 💡Note: 

Most words change their role depending on context:

👉 Example:

- **Fast** (adjective) → That is a fast car.

- **Fast** (adverb) → He runs fast.

## ✅ Lesson 3: Subject-Verb Agreement

**Subject-verb agreement** means the **subject and verb** must match in **number**:

- Singular subject → singular verb  
- Plural subject → plural verb  

👉 Examples:  
- *The dog barks.* (singular)   
- *The dogs bark.* (plural)

### 1️⃣ Basic Rule
**Singular subject** = verb **with -s**
**Plural subject** = verb **without -s**

👉 Examples:

- The **student writes** neatly. (singular)
- The **students write** neatly. (plural)

### 2️⃣ Compound Subjects

When two subjects are joined by **and**, use **a plural verb**.

👉 Examples:
- Tom and Jerry **are** friends.
- The teacher and the principal **have** arrived.

### 3️⃣ Subjects Joined by “Or” or “Nor”

The verb agrees with the subject **closest to it.**

👉 Examples:
- Either the manager or the employees **are** responsible.
- Neither the students nor the teacher **is** late.

### 4️⃣ Indefinite Pronouns

Some indefinite pronouns are singular, some plural.

**Always singular:**

anyone, everyone, someone, no one, nobody, each, either, neither

👉 Examples:
- Everyone **is** here.
- Neither of the answers **is** correct.

**Always plural:**

few, many, several, both

👉 Examples:
- Few **are** chosen.
- Both **were** accepted.

### 5️⃣ Collective Nouns

A group acting as one = singular.
A group acting as individuals = plural.

👉 Examples:
- The team **is** winning. (one team)
- The team **are** arguing among themselves. (individual players)

### 6️⃣ Special Cases
Be careful with:

👉 Titles of books/movies → singular
- The Avengers is a popular movie.

👉 Distances, money, time → singular
- Five dollars is enough.
- Two hours is a long time. 

👉 More
- Each of the students **is** ready.
- Neither he nor his friends **are** coming.

### 💡Note: 

Always find the real subject of the sentence, not the words in between.

👉 Example:
- **The bouquet** of flowers is beautiful. (bouquet is singular)

## ✅ Lesson 4: Tense Consistency

**Tense consistency** means that your verbs should stay in the same time frame within a sentence or paragraph unless you have a clear reason to change them.


| Tense                      | Structure                                  | Example                            |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Present Simple**        | Subject + base verb (+s/es for he/she/it)   | She writes essays.                 |
| **Past Simple**           | Subject + past form of verb                 | He walked to class.                |
| **Future Simple**         | Subject + will + base verb                  | They will arrive soon.             |
| **Present Continuous**    | Subject + am/is/are + verb(-ing)            | I am reading a book.               |
| **Past Continuous**       | Subject + was/were + verb(-ing)             | We were watching TV.               |
| **Future Continuous**     | Subject + will be + verb(-ing)              | She will be working tomorrow.      |
| **Present Perfect**       | Subject + has/have + past participle        | He has finished his homework.      |
| **Past Perfect**          | Subject + had + past participle             | They had left before sunset.       |
| **Future Perfect**        | Subject + will have + past participle       | I will have submitted the paper.   |
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | Subject + has/have been + verb(-ing)    | She has been studying all day.     |
| **Past Perfect Continuous**    | Subject + had been + verb(-ing)         | He had been working for hours.     |
| **Future Perfect Continuous**  | Subject + will have been + verb(-ing)   | They will have been driving all night. |


### 💡Note: 

- **Simple tenses** = facts, regular actions, completed actions.

- **Continuous tenses** = ongoing actions.

- **Perfect tenses** = link two time periods or focus on result.

- **Perfect Continuous tenses** = ongoing actions up to a point.

## ✅ Lesson 5: Articles

- **A** = **Indefinite article** before consonant sounds → *a book*  
- **An** = **Indefinite article** before vowel sounds (a, e, i, o, u) → *an apple*  
- **The** = **Definite article** specific or known noun → *the sun*  

### 1️⃣ A / An (Indefinite Articles)

Use “a” or “an” when talking about something not specific or mentioned for the first time.

👉 Examples:
- I saw **a cat** in the garden.
- She bought **an apple** for lunch.
- He is **a university** student. (note: "university" starts with a consonant sound **“yu”**)

👉 Remember:
- It’s about sound, not just the letter.
- **An hour** (because “hour” sounds like “our” → vowel sound).

### 2️⃣ The (Definite Article)

Use “the” when the noun is specific, known, or unique.

👉 Examples:
- I sat on **the chair** next to the window.
- **The sun** rises in the east.
- He met **the president** yesterday.

👉 Special cases:
- **The Himalayas, the Amazon River, the United States**

### 3️⃣ No Article (Zero Article)

Some nouns **do not** take an article:

- **Names** → I visited **Bangladesh**.

- **Languages** → She speaks **English**.

- **Meals** → We have **lunch** at noon.

- **Sports** → He plays **football**.

- **Abstract ideas** → **Freedom** is important.

## ✅ Lesson 6: Sentence Fragments & Run-ons

## 1️⃣ Sentence Fragments

**A sentence fragment** is an incomplete sentence. It’s missing a subject, a verb, or a complete thought.

👉 Examples ❌:
- Because I was tired.
- After the meeting.
- When he arrived.

**Why the above examples are wrong?** These do not express a full idea.


👉 Examples ✅:
- I went to bed because I was tired.
- We had lunch after the meeting.
- When he arrived, we started the presentation.

### 2️⃣ Run-on Sentences

A run-on sentence incorrectly joins two (or more) independent clauses without proper punctuation or a conjunction.

👉 Example ❌:
- I love reading I visit the library often.

👉 Example ✅: 

1. Separate into two sentences:
- I love reading. I visit the library often.

2. Use a semicolon:
- I love reading; I visit the library often.

3. Use a conjunction:
- I love reading, and I visit the library often.

## ✅ Lesson 7: Punctuation

| Mark | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| .    | End of sentence | *He left.* |
| ,    | Pause/list/link | *Apples, bananas, and grapes.* |
| ;    | Link related clauses | *I was tired; I stayed in.* |
| :    | Intro to list | *Bring the following: pens, books.* |
| '    | Possession/contraction | *It's cold.* / *John's hat* |
| " "  | Quotation | *He said, “Hello.”* |
| ?    | Question | *Are you okay?* |
| ()   | Extra info | *He (the manager) agreed.* |
| —    | Emphasis | *He won — against all odds.* |

## ✅ Lesson 8: Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

- Match the **pronoun** with the **noun** (antecedent) in **number and gender**  

👉 Example:
- Incorrect: Every student must bring **their** book. ❌
- Correct: Every student must bring **his or her** book. ✅

### 1️⃣ Singular vs. Plural Agreement
|Noun (Antecedent)|Correct Pronoun|
|-----------------|---------------|
|   The student   | he/she/his/her |
|   The teacher   | they/them/their |

👉 Examples:
- The teacher gave **her** notes to the class. ✅
- The students submitted **their** assignments. ✅

### 2️⃣ Indefinite Pronouns

Some pronouns sound plural but are actually singular:

- **Each, everyone, anybody, neither, either, someone, nobody, etc.**

👉 Examples:
- Everyone brought **his** or her notebook. ✅
- Each of the boys did **his** homework. ✅

### 3️⃣ Plural Pronouns

Some are always plural:

- **Many, several, both, few**

👉 Examples:
- Several students forgot **their** ID cards. ✅
- Both are bringing **their** laptops. ✅

### 4️⃣ Gender-Neutral Option (Modern Style)

In formal writing, if gender is unknown or irrelevant, many writers now use **they** as a singular pronoun.

👉 Example:
- If a student forgets the password, **they** should contact IT.

### 5️⃣ Collective Nouns

A collective noun (like team, class, company) can be:

- Singular if acting as one unit

- Plural if emphasizing individuals

👉 Examples:
- The team won **its** (*indicate ownership*) game. ✅
- The team argued among **themselves**. ✅

## ✅ Lesson 9: Parallelism

Parallelism means using **the same grammatical structure** for items in a list or parts of a sentence that are joined by conjunctions (and, or, but, etc.).

If one element is a noun, the others should be nouns. If one is a verb in "-ing" form, the rest should match.

❌ Wrong (not parallel):
- I enjoy reading, to write, and watching movies.

✅ Correct (parallel):
- I enjoy **reading, writing, and watching** movies.

## ✅ Lesson 10: Active vs. Passive Voice

**Voice** shows whether the subject of a sentence performs the action (**active**) or receives it (**passive**).

| Voice | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Active | Subject + verb + object | *She wrote the essay.* |
| Passive | Object + be + past participle + (by subject) | *The essay was written by her.* |

## ✅ Lesson 11: Sentence Patterns

A **sentence pattern** is the structure or order in which the elements of a sentence appear.
Understanding these helps you build clear, flexible, and sophisticated sentences.

There are **5 core sentence patterns** in English based on the functions of **Subject (S)**, **Verb (V)**, **Object (O)**, **Complement (C)**, and **Indirect Object (IO)**.

### 1️⃣ S + V (Subject + Verb)

Used for intransitive verbs (no object).

👉 Examples: 
- The baby **cried**.
- They **slept** peacefully.

### 2️⃣ S + V + O (Subject + Verb + Object)

Used with transitive verbs (needs an object).

👉 Examples: 
- She **reads** books.
- I **watched** the movie.

### 3️⃣ S + V + IO + DO (Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object)

The indirect object is the person/thing receiving the direct object.

👉 Examples: 
- He **gave** me (IO) a gift (DO).
- The teacher **taught** us (IO) a lesson (DO).

### 4️⃣ S + V + C (Subject + Verb + Complement)

Used with linking verbs (be, seem, become, etc.)
Complements describe or identify the subject.

👉 Examples:
- She **is** a doctor.
- The food **smells** delicious.

### 5️⃣ S + V + O + C (Subject + Verb + Object + Complement)

The complement refers to the object, not the subject.

👉 Examples:
- They **elected** her (O) president (C).
- We **painted** the walls (O) blue (C).

## ✅ Lesson 12: Clauses and Phrases

### What is a Phrase?

A **phrase** is a group of words that **does not contain both a subject and a verb**. It acts as a single part of speech.

#### Types of Phrases:

- **Noun Phrase:** the big red ball  
- **Verb Phrase:** is running fast  
- **Prepositional Phrase:** on the table  
- **Infinitive Phrase:** to win the game  
- **Gerund Phrase:** swimming in the ocean

👉 Examples:
- I saw **a dog with a red collar**. (noun phrase)  
- He is **running late**. (verb phrase)  
- **After dinner**, we went for a walk. (prepositional phrase)



### What is a Clause?

A **clause** contains **both a subject and a verb**.

#### Types of Clauses:

- **Independent Clause:** Can stand alone as a full sentence  
  - *I enjoy reading.*  
  - *She went home.*

- **Dependent (Subordinate) Clause:** Cannot stand alone; needs an independent clause  
  - *Because I was tired*  
  - *When he arrived*  
  - *Although she passed*

👉 Examples:
- *Because I was tired, I went to sleep.*  
- *I stayed inside while it was raining.*

---

### ✅ Combining Clauses and Phrases

You can use both in one sentence:

👉 Examples:
- *After the exam*, she went home *because she was tired*.

This sentence contains:
- **Prepositional Phrase:** After the exam  
- **Independent Clause:** she went home  
- **Dependent Clause:** because she was tired

## ✅ Lesson 13: Modifiers

- **Adjectives:** modify nouns → *a red car*  
- **Adverbs:** modify verbs/adjectives → *runs quickly*  
- **Phrases as modifiers:** *The boy **with the backpack** is my friend.*

**Avoid:**  
- **Misplaced modifiers** → *She almost drove her kids every day.*  
- **Correct:** *She drove her kids almost every day.*
