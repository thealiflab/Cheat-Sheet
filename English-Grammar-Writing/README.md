# 📘 Mastering English Grammar for Essay Writing

A complete grammar course covering all essential topics for writing error-free, professional English — ideal for students, professionals, and essay writers.

---

## ✅ Lesson 1: Sentence Structure

**Simple Sentence:** One independent clause  
- *I read books.*

**Compound Sentence:** Two independent clauses + FANBOYS  
- *I read books, and I write essays.*

**Complex Sentence:** One independent + one dependent clause  
- *Because I was tired, I went to sleep.*

**Compound-Complex Sentence:** At least two independent + one dependent clause  
- *Although I was tired, I finished my homework, and I submitted it.*

## ✅ Lesson 3: Subject-Verb Agreement

- Singular subject → singular verb  
- Plural subject → plural verb  

**Example:**  
- *The dog barks.*  
- *The dogs bark.*

Special cases:  
- *Each of the students **is** ready.*  
- *Neither he nor his friends **are** coming.*

---

## ✅ Lesson 4: Tense Consistency

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Present Simple | I study |
| Past Simple | I studied |
| Future Simple | I will study |
| Present Continuous | I am studying |
| Past Perfect | I had studied |
| Future Perfect | I will have studied |

**Avoid switching tenses unnecessarily.**

---

## ✅ Lesson 5: Articles

- **A** = before consonant sounds → *a book*  
- **An** = before vowel sounds → *an apple*  
- **The** = specific or known noun → *the sun*  

---

## ✅ Lesson 6: Sentence Fragments & Run-ons

- **Fragment:** *Because I was late.* ❌  
- **Run-on:** *I studied hard I passed the test.* ❌  
- **Correct:** *Because I was late, I missed the bus.* ✅  
- Use punctuation or conjunctions to join independent clauses.

---

## ✅ Lesson 7: Punctuation

| Mark | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| .    | End of sentence | *He left.* |
| ,    | Pause/list/link | *Apples, bananas, and grapes.* |
| ;    | Link related clauses | *I was tired; I stayed in.* |
| :    | Intro to list | *Bring the following: pens, books.* |
| '    | Possession/contraction | *It's cold.* / *John's hat* |
| " "  | Quotation | *He said, “Hello.”* |
| ?    | Question | *Are you okay?* |
| ()   | Extra info | *He (the manager) agreed.* |
| —    | Emphasis | *He won — against all odds.* |

---

## ✅ Lesson 8: Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

- Match the **pronoun** with the **noun** (antecedent) in **number and gender**  
- *Each student must bring **his or her** notebook.*  
- Gender-neutral: *A person should check **their** answers.* ✅

---

## ✅ Lesson 9: Parallelism

Use consistent grammatical forms in lists or comparisons.

❌ *I like swimming, to run, and biking.*  
✅ *I like swimming, running, and biking.*

With conjunctions:  
- *She is not only smart but also hardworking.*

---

## ✅ Lesson 10: Active vs. Passive Voice

| Voice | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Active | Subject + verb + object | *She wrote the essay.* |
| Passive | Object + be + past participle + (by subject) | *The essay was written by her.* |

Use **active voice** in essays for clarity and strength.

## ✅ Lesson 12: Clauses and Phrases

- **Phrase:** No subject-verb → *on the table*  
- **Clause:** Has subject-verb → *because I was tired*

Types:  
- **Independent clause:** *She reads daily.*  
- **Dependent clause:** *Although she was tired…*

---

## ✅ Lesson 13: Modifiers

- **Adjectives:** modify nouns → *a red car*  
- **Adverbs:** modify verbs/adjectives → *runs quickly*  
- **Phrases as modifiers:** *The boy **with the backpack** is my friend.*

**Avoid:**  
- **Misplaced modifiers** → *She almost drove her kids every day.*  
- **Correct:** *She drove her kids almost every day.*

## ✅ Lesson 14: Commonly Confused Words

| Word Pair | Usage Example |
|-----------|----------------|
| it’s / its | *It’s raining.* / *Its tail is long.* |
| your / you’re | *Your book.* / *You’re smart.* |
| their / there / they’re | *Their house.* / *There is a cat.* / *They’re happy.* |
| then / than | *Eat, then sleep.* / *Faster than you.* |
| affect / effect | *It affects me.* / *The effect was strong.* |

## ✅ Lesson 15: Transition Words for Essays

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Addition | *Moreover, in addition, also* |
| Contrast | *However, although, on the other hand* |
| Cause & Effect | *Therefore, because, as a result* |
| Comparison | *Similarly, likewise, just as* |
| Emphasis | *Indeed, clearly, obviously* |
| Time / Sequence | *First, next, then, finally* |
| Conclusion | *In conclusion, to sum up, overall* |

**Example:**  
- *First, gather your sources. Then, begin drafting your ideas. Finally, revise and submit your essay.*
